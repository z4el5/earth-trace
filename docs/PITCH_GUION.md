## 1 · Gancho
**EN:** "In the southwest of the Dominican Republic, a lake grew so much it swallowed roads, homes and farms. NASA satellites saw all of it. But almost nobody knows how to read that evidence. We are EARTH//TRACE."
*ES: Abrir con la historia del Lago Enriquillo. Pausa antes del nombre.*

## 2 · Problema
**EN:** "NASA data is free and huge, but it was built for specialists. People face three barriers: too much data, not knowing if a change is real or just noise, and contradictions between regions that stay invisible. Lake Enriquillo grew while the Aral Sea shrank: same variable, opposite stories."

## 3 · Solución
**EN:** "EARTH//TRACE answers four questions about any place on Earth: what changes, where, how much, and whether it is significant. Let me show you." → **Demo:** open Earth Explorer, tap the Dominican Republic (passport stamp + XP), slide the before/after satellite view, ask "What is happening to Lake Enriquillo?", then open Game case 1 and jump to the Lab verdict.

## 4 · Ciencia
**EN:** "Behind every answer there is real NASA data and real statistics: Landsat, MODIS, GPM, SMAP, GRACE-FO and SWOT, processed in Python and tested with the Mann-Kendall test and Sen’s slope. Think of a player: one good game doesn’t prove they’re improving; most games must beat the earlier ones. Every verdict states its limits."

## 5 · Diferenciación
**EN:** "It is gamified from start to finish, it detects contradictions automatically, you can ask it anything in plain language, and it is open: an API, a plugin any school can embed, and a Skill that teaches AI assistants to use our data."

## 6 · Impacto
**EN:** "Today it covers 110 countries on every continent, 17 satellite datasets, in English and Spanish. Students learn by investigating, communities see their own territory, and media and developers reuse verified data."

## 7 · Cierre
**EN:** "The Earth is constantly changing. We turn those changes into discoveries. Open the demo and earn your first passport stamp."
